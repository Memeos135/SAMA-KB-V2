# Bank Account Rules

## Why this community

The bank account lifecycle regime: who may open an account, what evidence and due diligence is required by customer type, and how accounts are operated, frozen, made dormant, disclosed or enforced against. This is the principal intersection of consumer/account-opening rules with AML/CTF customer due diligence.

## How members connect

- The Rules for Bank Accounts is the hub instrument; the natural-person (200), juristic-person (300), foreign-investor (400) and operations (Chapter IV) parts are subordinate chapters that all reference back to it, with sub-rules (expatriates, Hajj/Umrah entities, NPOs, multilateral organisations, liquidation/restructuring, non-residents) nested beneath the juristic-person rules.
- KYC and AML/CTF requirements are incorporated into the account-opening rules, so the account-type documentation lists function as the operational expression of CDD obligations — the 'account holder' under these rules must also be assessed as the 'customer' under the AML regime.
- Statutory cross-references define eligibility and restrictions: the Companies Law and Foreign Investment Law govern juristic-person capacity and foreign investor scope; the Bankruptcy Law drives the liquidation/restructuring account category; the Law of Terrorism Crimes and Financing underpins freezing and non-dealing duties.
- Supervisory/administrative bodies act as documentary gatekeepers — licences and registrations issued by the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah, HRSD, the General Authority for Awqaf and the MOI ten-digit number are conditions precedent to opening specified account types.
- Freezing, dormancy/unclaimed balances, disclosure and enforcement obligations form a downstream chain applying once the account is live, with the electronic record system as the shared evidentiary layer.

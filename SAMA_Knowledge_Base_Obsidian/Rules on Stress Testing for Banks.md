# Rules on Stress Testing for Banks

The SAMA Rules on Stress Testing (issued November 2011 to all banks under SAMA's charter powers) set minimum regulatory requirements for bank stress testing as a risk management tool, covering framework design, methodologies, scenario selection, board and senior management responsibility, and half-yearly reporting to SAMA. Section 12 makes stress testing programs subject to supervisory review, ICAAP bilateral meetings and on-site examinations, assessing framework adequacy, assumptions, frequency, governance involvement and degree of compliance. It binds locally incorporated banks and branches of foreign banks above the specified asset threshold; banks may adopt more sophisticated techniques than the stated minimums.

**Regimes:** governance/risk, banking prudential

### [[Credit Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** If credit exposures are booked through Shari'ah compliant structures, they still fall inside the stress testing perimeter. The Stress Testing Rules require a holistic approach covering all material risks the bank is or could be exposed to, and the Shari'ah framework identifies credit risk as one of the risks generated by Shari'ah compliant banking activities — so the Shari'ah-specific features of those exposures are not a carve-out but an input to scenario design and coverage. The consequence is that a stress testing programme that omits or generically aggregates Shari'ah compliant credit exposures would be incomplete against the holistic-coverage requirement.

### [[Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Plan (ICAAP)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When a bank runs its annual SNCR (step-in / non-contractual risk) assessment, it must decide whether the ICAAP submission also discharges its stress-testing reporting duty — it does not. The ICAAP obligation and the Rules on Stress Testing are separate reporting channels serving different regulatory purposes: ICAAP output feeds capital planning and internal capital adequacy, while the stress-testing submission is directed at SAMA's evaluation of the robustness of the bank's stress-testing framework itself. Practically, the reader should plan for two distinct deliverables with different content and audiences, even where the underlying scenario analysis overlaps.

### [[Operational Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Operational risk is not exempt from the stress testing obligation simply because it is managed under a separate operational risk regime.

# Derivative Exposures Treatment

Covers the prudential treatment and disclosure of derivative exposures within the credit risk framework, including how collateral, financial guarantees and credit derivatives are recognised as credit risk mitigation (template CR3 and its worked allocation examples) and how problem assets are disclosed (table CRB-A, referencing SAMA's Rules on Management of Problem Assets, January 2020). Sets out definitions of unsecured versus secured carrying amounts and the ordering rules when an exposure benefits from multiple mitigants. Mandatory for all SAMA-supervised banks regardless of whether the standardised or IRB approach is used.

**Regimes:** banking prudential

### [[Leverage Ratio Exposure Measure]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Derivative exposures are one of the enumerated categories that must be captured in the leverage ratio exposure measure, so any question about whether a derivative position is in scope is answered by reading the derivative-specific rule as a component of the broader exposure measure provision. The relationship is hierarchical within one instrument: the exposure measure sets the perimeter (on-balance sheet, derivatives, SFTs, off-balance sheet items) and the derivative provision specifies how that category is quantified. For a compliance decision this means exclusions or netting claimed at the derivative level must still be reconciled against the exposure measure's overall scoping rules.

### [[Potential Future Exposure (PFE)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Deciding the leverage ratio treatment of a derivative position requires both components: the current exposure measure and the potential future exposure add-on, which are specified in the same instrument. The derivative exposures provision is the operative rule and PFE is the defined input it depends on, so the two form a single obligation chain rather than alternatives. The consequence is that omitting or mis-scoping PFE understates the derivative component of the exposure measure and therefore overstates the leverage ratio.

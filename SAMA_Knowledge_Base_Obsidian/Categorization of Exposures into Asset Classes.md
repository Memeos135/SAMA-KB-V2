# Categorization of Exposures into Asset Classes

Sets out how banks using the Internal Ratings-Based (IRB) approach must sort banking-book exposures into the asset classes of corporate, sovereign, bank, retail and equity, with five specialized lending sub-classes within corporate, three sub-classes within retail, and possible purchased receivables treatment; the IRB approach is not permitted for equity. Banks must apply the correct class-based treatment for minimum capital purposes and must demonstrate to SAMA that their assignment methodology is appropriate and consistently applied over time. Binds SAMA-supervised banks using IRB.

**Regimes:** banking prudential, governance/risk

### [[IRB Approach Overview|IRB Approach: Overview]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Before a bank can compute any IRB capital number it must first decide which asset class an exposure falls into, and this categorization provision is the entry gate to the IRB approach rather than a standalone rule. Both sit within the same minimum capital requirements framework, so the asset-class definitions (corporate, sovereign, bank, retail and their sub-classes) determine which IRB risk-weight function, parameter estimation duties and minimum requirements attach. In practice, a misclassification at this step propagates into the wrong RWA treatment, so classification evidence should be documented before the modelling approach is defended.

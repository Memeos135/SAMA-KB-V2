# Specialized Lending (SL)

Sets out the specialized lending (SL) subcategory of corporate exposures, defined by reliance on the income of a financed asset or project rather than the borrower's broader capacity to repay. Under the standardized approach it distinguishes project finance, object finance and commodities finance, and requires use of issue-specific external ratings only (issuer ratings are not permitted); the IRB chapters add income-producing real estate and HVCRE and a supervisory slotting approach. Binds SAMA-supervised banks in classifying and risk-weighting such exposures.

**Regimes:** banking prudential

### [[Exposures to Corporates]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a corporate facility, the reader must check whether it is in fact specialized lending, because SL is a defined sub-category carved out of the corporate asset class rather than a separate regime. The corporate exposure definition feeds directly into the SL sub-classes (project, object, commodities finance, income-producing real estate and high-volatility commercial real estate), each of which has its own risk-weight and expected-loss treatment, including the supervisory slotting approach where the bank cannot meet the estimation requirements. Consequently the general corporate IRB treatment cannot be applied to an SL exposure without first ruling out the sub-class criteria and the slotting fallback.

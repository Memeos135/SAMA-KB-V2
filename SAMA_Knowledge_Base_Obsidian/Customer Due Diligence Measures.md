# Customer Due Diligence Measures

Sets out the customer due diligence obligations owed by financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions (DNFBPs), including the triggers for applying CDD (account opening or establishing a business relationship, occasional and linked transactions, wire transfers for non-relationship customers, suspicion of terrorism financing, and doubts about the adequacy of customer particulars). It covers risk-based calibration of measures, ongoing monitoring and record refresh for existing customers and beneficial owners, limited postponement of identity verification subject to risk controls, and the mandatory consequences where CDD cannot be completed (refuse the account/relationship/transaction, terminate existing relationships, and report to the General Directorate of Financial Intelligence). It binds all reporting entities supervised under the AML/CTF framework, including SAMA-supervised financial institutions.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, sanctions/TFS, governance/risk

- **What this link tells you:** For a remittance centre deciding whether its onboarding and monitoring controls are adequate, these two provisions of the same rulebook operate as one obligation chain and cannot be satisfied separately. The KYC principle sets the objective — establishing the true identity of the customer and understanding the expected business and transaction profile — while the CDD measures set the operative duties, including verifying that documents, data and information remain relevant and up to date, with intensified attention to high-risk customers and application to existing customers and beneficial owners.

# Anti-Fraud Framework

## Why this community

SAMA's counter-fraud regime for supervised institutions: the mandatory governance, prevention, detection and response obligations that sit alongside (and interlock with) the cyber security and IT governance frameworks.

## How members connect

- Hierarchy: the Counter-Fraud Framework and its fundamental requirements sit above the four domains (Governance, Prevent, Detect, Respond), each of which decomposes into named controls such as fraud risk assessment, multi-factor authentication, mule account detection and the fraud response plan.
- Obligation chain: board-level fraud risk appetite drives the fraud risk assessment, which drives key risk indicators, intelligence monitoring and the maturity model used for SAMA supervisory assessment and notification duties.
- Accountability structure is defined by role: Counter-Fraud Governance Committee, Head of Counter-Fraud and the Counter-Fraud Department, with whistle-blowing and supervisory notification as escalation routes.
- Cross-regime linkage: authentication, system development, change security and IT project management controls are cited from the SAMA Cyber Security and IT Governance Frameworks, so fraud controls inherit those requirements; sector-specific application to finance companies comes via the Anti-Fraud Rules circular.
